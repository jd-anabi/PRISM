Figure and table. This look sets PRISM the way a careful paper sets its results: black ink on a clear page with no boxes or tinted panels, every list of settings or records set as a ruled table, one small marker shape for each state, and progress drawn as a bar on a labelled time axis whose hatched end is the honest range for when the work should finish. It comes from the scientific page itself: the table with three rules, the figure labelled directly with its caption beneath, and the colour-blind-safe colours scientists already use in figures, set in the lettering PRISM already carries (Inter), with a book face (Cambria, which comes with Windows) kept for four places only: the line under a page heading, a figure caption, a message and the question in a dialog. Working in it is quiet and exact: a state is always a shape and a word, never a colour alone; the main button always sits at the bottom right, beside the list of facts that must hold before a start; and one line of words above the time axis always says where things stand (screens 3 to 7 show the run page on an ordinary day, the form locked during a training, the key to every marker, and both screens at the smallest window). Its one honest risk is that it has almost no colour or panels to lean on: it stays clear only if every later screen is set with the same care over spacing, rules and wording, and the book face, so far seen only in a browser and not in the program itself, is the part most likely to need changing (it can be swapped for Inter without touching anything else).
