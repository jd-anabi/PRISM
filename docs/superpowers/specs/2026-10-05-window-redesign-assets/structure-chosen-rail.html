The smallest window, 900 by 600

Screen 2 drawn at that size. A person with Windows text at 150 % on a laptop has about this much room. Four rules, the same on every screen:

  1. The rail keeps its names and its width. The page's side padding drops from 32 to 20 px.
  2. Below about 1,100 px of window each step is one line: its number, a short name and its state icon. The state's words move into the heading, here "Training: ready to start".
  3. Two columns become one that scrolls. The row with the main action is pinned under it, with the two facts that matter most, so it is always in view.
  4. The strip gives up the run's name first, then the time left. Whether the run needs you, and "Go to the run", never go.

Rule 4, on any page but the run's own:all is fine, and the run needs you.

Getting around by keyboard

Screen 3 at 900 by 600. The keys are the same at every size, and Help lists them:

  1. F6 moves between the rail, the page and the strip.
  2. The rail is one list and the six steps are one tab bar: Tab reaches each once, the arrow keys move within it, Enter opens.
  3. Ctrl+1 to Ctrl+6 open Home and the parts down to Records. Alt+1 to Alt+6 open the six steps. Ctrl+R goes to the run.
  4. The same 2 px outline marks where the keyboard is: on a rail entry (screen 1), a field (2), a flat button (3), a step (4) and a table row (5).
  5. On arrival the keyboard is on the first setting, never on a button that starts or stops a run, and Enter in a field starts nothing.

Nothing is left out at this size: the rest of the health figures, "If you stop now", the full log and the settings follow in the same scrolling column.

Every part of PRISM sits on a rail at the left that never goes away, a strip along the bottom says in one sentence whether a run is live and whether it needs you, and Home is a status page: what to do next, where the six inference steps stand, what each part is for and the latest records. You change part with one click on the rail, move through Parameter inference on its track of six steps (each can be opened at any time and says what it is waiting for), and reach a live run from anywhere with "Go to the run" on the strip. Its one honest weakness is that Home, the step track and Records state the same facts in three places, so each sentence has to be read from one record, and the one that says how a run ended does not exist yet: PRISM would have to start writing a short line when a run starts and again when it ends. Two small things here were not asked for and would be new, that short line about each run and a Records list that can show all kinds at once; screens 8 and 9 are sheets of states and not screens of the program, 10 and 11 are the smallest window, and the look is plain on purpose because it is being decided separately.
