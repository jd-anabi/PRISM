# Where the models' math lives — a map for the audit, 2026-10-05

A working document for the code audit, which will check the code against **a PDF of the models that
the owner will supply**. It says where each piece of math is implemented, where the same equation
is written more than once and must agree, which conventions have to be pinned down before any
formula is compared, which checks can be made on this machine without trusting the code, and what
to ask the owner about the PDF. It also records three leads found while mapping, the first of
which was then reproduced with the program's own functions.

**How this was established.** One reader went through the files named below on 2026-10-05 without
running PRISM (two scratch scripts used sympy, numpy and scipy only). Its map has not been checked
by a second reader: treat every line as a pointer to verify, not as a finding. The exception is
lead 1 in the next section, which was reproduced afterwards on the CPU with `core.forcing`,
`core.Helpers.helpers` and `core.SBI.chi` themselves. The code had not changed since `3ec5bf6`.

**The starting fact.** There is no written statement of the models anywhere in the repository or
its archive today: no PDF, LaTeX or notebook, and `docs/guide/science.md` states only the three
Nadrowski noise amplitudes, the tier-1 relation, the thermal-tail formula and the unit
conventions. It never states the drift equations, the open probability, what each
non-dimensional parameter means, or the reduction's formulas. Each model's equations are written
two to four times in the code, and the tests pin those copies against each other, never against an
independent statement. The owner's PDF will be the first independent statement the code has been
checked against.

## 1. Three leads found while mapping

### Lead 1 — model time is put on the recording's clock by a whole-number stride (REPRODUCED)

*What the code does.* A simulation runs on one fine grid whose step is the recording's frame
interval over the largest time scale in the box (`dt_exp / t_scale_hi`, 0.025 on the master
boxes). A cell's own time scale then enters in two places that use different arithmetic:

- the **drive** is built in the cell's physical time, `t_dim = t_scale * t_nd`, with the
  continuous `t_scale` (`core/forcing.py:171`);
- the **response** is put on the recording's clock by keeping every `sub`-th fine point, with
  `sub = max(1, round((dt_exp / t_scale) / dt_nd_min))` (`core/SBI/pipeline.py:1654-1657`; the
  probe stage's own stride at `core/SBI/chi_probes.py:186-191`), and every later step treats the
  kept points as exactly `dt_exp` apart (`core/SBI/chi.py:463`, the lock-in's `arange * dt`).

The kept points are really `dt_exp * sub / (t_scale_hi / t_scale)` apart. That equals `dt_exp`
only when `t_scale_hi / t_scale` is a whole number.

*Two consequences.*

1. **Every simulated trace is the trace of a cell whose time scale is `t_scale_hi / sub`**, one of
   40, 20, 13.3, 10, 8, 6.67, … on the master boxes, while the row is labelled with the continuous
   `t_scale` it was drawn at. At `t_scale` 25 the trace is a cell at 20; at 30 it is a cell at 40.
2. **The drive is detuned from the lock-in that measures the response**, by the same factor, so
   the measured susceptibility is attenuated by an amount that depends on the cell's time scale
   and on how many drive cycles are locked in. A bench recording has no such detuning.

*The reproduction (2026-10-05, CPU, float64).* A system whose response equals its drive, so that
the true answer is 1 at every frequency and length, was pushed through the real drive builder,
the pipeline's own stride line, the real rescale and the real `lock_in_batched`, at a 100 ms drive
period:

| 1, 2, 4, 5, 8, 10, 20, 40 | whole | exact | 1.0000 | 1.000 | 1.000 | 1.000 | 1.000 | 1.000 | 1.000 |
| 3.73 (the master cell) | 10.724 | 11 | 1.0257 | 0.983 | 0.963 | 0.891 | 0.625 | 0.029 | 0.999 |
| 7.215 | 5.544 | 6 | 1.0823 | 0.924 | 0.759 | 0.205 | 0.180 | 0.076 | 0.994 |

The entries are |χ| as a fraction of the true value. With the lock-in given the true spacing of
the kept points (the last column) the answer is 1 in every row, so the loss is the clock and
nothing else. At the master cell the lock-in reads nothing at about 39 drive cycles
(1 / 0.0257): `docs/guide/science.md` ("Chi probe design") measured a "wall" at 32 to 36 cycles on
that cell, set the 20-cycle lock-in ceiling to stay inside it, and records the mechanism as not
established. This may be that mechanism; that link is an inference and has not been tested.

*What was and was not shown.* Shown: the arithmetic of the real functions, for an ideal response.
Not shown: an end-to-end training batch (there the probe frequencies are multiples of a peak
frequency measured on the same mis-clocked trace, which moves the drive relative to the cell's
resonance as well); the size of the effect on a real bundle's response; whether forced mode's
features and the FDT path are affected in the same way (the FDT lock-in uses absolute time and may
not be); and what "the model at `t_scale`" is meant to be, which is the owner's to say. No fix has
been designed.

*What follows if it stands.* A simulated row holds something else once this is fixed, and the
simulation cache's key does not change with the code, so the finished cache
(`Artifacts/simulations/eda5c2a1e468`) could not be trained on. A calibration on simulated data
cannot see this fault: it draws its test sets from the same simulator.

### Lead 2 — the drive's clock starts before the discarded transient (not reproduced)

The drive is built on the fine grid from non-dimensional time 0; the first 4,000 fine steps (100
time units) are discarded; and both lock-ins of the inference path count time from the first kept
sample (`core/SBI/chi.py:463`, `core/SBI/statistics.py:414`). Simulated phases therefore carry an
extra `2π · f · t_scale · 100` that a bench recording does not. The bench paths take sample 0 as
time 0; forced mode asks for the drive's phase, chi mode asks for none
(`core/SBI/observations.py:192-300`). The statistics module's docstring treats local time as
deliberate. The FDT lock-in uses absolute time and has no such offset.

### Lead 3 — the solver's own step drifts between segments (not reproduced)

The solver takes its step as `t[1] - t[0]` of a float32 `linspace` over each segment's absolute end
points (`core/Solvers/sdeint.py:199-200`, `:267-269`). A numpy emulation gives exactly 0.025 in the
first segment and 0.0250244 (+0.10 %) or 0.0249023 (−0.39 %) in later ones, while the drive stays
on the nominal grid; each segment also starts from the previous one's last state, so one sample is
duplicated at each seam (`core/Simulator/simulator.py:92-99`). The emulation assumes how
`torch.linspace` rounds; it was not run with torch.

## 4. Conventions to pin down before any formula is compared

- Symbols against names, Nadrowski. File names k, lam, f_max, tau, tau_c, s, delta_E, beta, n, temp; constructor names differ for two (f, delta_e) and binding is by position; plot symbols are kappa, lambda-tilde, phi, tau-tilde, tau_c-tilde, S, Delta-G-tilde, beta, N, T-tilde (core/config.py:200-201). State variables x, y, c in code; x_init, xa_init, c_init in cell files. The dimensional meaning of each is written nowhere; the code and its comments imply k = K_sp/K_gs, lam = motor drag over bundle drag, f_max = F_max/(K_gs D), beta = K_gs D^2/(N k_B T), delta_E in units of k_B T, temp = T_a/T, tau and tau_c in units of t_scale. INFERRED, to be confirmed from the PDF.
- The three scales. Implied by comments only (core/sim_config.py:140, core/SBI/pipeline.py:1327-1328, core/Reduction/rescaling.py:41-43): x_scale = D (gating swing, nm), t_scale = bundle drag/K_gs (ms), f_scale = K_gs*D (pN). Under these the coded noise amplitudes, the tier-1 relation f_scale*x_scale = N beta k_B T, the thermal-tail formula at docs/guide/science.md:1091-1092 and the FDT prefactor N*beta are mutually consistent (scratch sympy check); the PDF must confirm the scales themselves. Offsets x_offset, t_offset, f_offset are optional and absent from the master boxes.
- How time is rescaled (LEAD, not yet reproduced with PRISM itself). There is no explicit time rescale: the trajectory lives on one fine grid of step dt_exp/t_scale_hi and is sub-sampled by sub = round(t_scale_hi/t_scale), so the true spacing of kept samples is t_scale*sub/t_scale_hi times dt_exp and equals dt_exp only when t_scale_hi/t_scale is an integer. Consequences to settle before any formula is compared: (i) the trajectory's effective time scale takes only the values t_scale_hi/n (40, 20, 13.3, 10, 8, ... on the master boxes) while the recorded training target is the continuous t_scale; (ii) the drive, built with the continuous t_scale (core/forcing.py:171), is detuned from every lock-in reference by that factor: 1.0258 at the master cell, up to roughly +/-1/(2n) in general. Numpy emulation of a system whose response equals its drive: \|chi\| 0.61 after 20 cycles and 0.07 after 142 cycles at t_scale 3.73, about 0 at 25 and 30, exactly 1 at 1, 2, 4, 5, 8, 10, 20, 40. Decide what 'the model at t_scale' means, then test.
- The drive's time origin and phase reference (LEAD). The drive is built on the fine grid from non-dimensional time 0, the first 4000 fine steps (100 time units) are discarded, and both SBI lock-ins count time from the first kept sample (chi.py:463, statistics.py:414). Simulated phases therefore carry an extra 2*pi*f*t_scale*100 (mod 2 pi); the statistics docstring (lines 22-26) treats local time as deliberate for t_offset. The bench paths take sample 0 as t = 0; forced mode asks for a phase, chi mode asks for none (core/SBI/observations.py:192-300). The FDT lock-in uses absolute time and has no such offset.
- Noise convention. The solver's g is an AMPLITUDE: dx = f dt + g dW. Hopf's sigma is that amplitude (D = sigma^2/2). User models type a STRENGTH D with correlation 2 D delta, so g = sqrt(2 D). Nadrowski's amplitudes are sqrt(2/(n beta)) and so on. The calcium noise is white, with strength 2 tau_c p(1-p)/n entering dc divided by tau; if the PDF has an exponentially correlated channel noise with time tau_c, the code is its white-noise limit and the PDF must say which is meant.
- Ito against Stratonovich. The scheme is Euler-Maruyama with g evaluated at the start of the step (Ito). For Nadrowski the calcium noise depends on x and y but not on c, so the two readings coincide; for a user model whose D names its own variable they do not.
- Fourier and susceptibility conventions. FDT and chi: chi = (2/(F0 T)) integral of x exp(+i omega t), drive F0 cos(omega t), response Re[chi F0 exp(-i omega t)], so chi = chi' + i chi'' with chi'' positive for dissipation (core/FDT/plots.py:229-232 says so in words). Group G: R_k uses exp(-i k omega t) and a drive amp*sin(2 pi f t + phase), so arg R1 - phase is the response lag minus pi/2. Chi probes are cosines (phase pi/2 in the sine builder, chi_probes.py:262). No test pins any of these signs.
- Spectrum convention. FDT's G is a one-sided density per unit ORDINARY frequency (scipy's Welch) tabulated against ANGULAR frequency, with G = 2 C(omega) and C(omega) the integral of exp(-i omega tau) <X(tau)X(0)> with no 1/(2 pi) (spectral.py:13-19). The ratio is prefactor*omega*G/(4 chi''). A PDF that defines the spectrum per radian, or two-sided, differs by factors of 2 and 2 pi.
- Frequency units. The inference path uses ordinary frequency in cycles per cell time unit (kHz in a millisecond cell), named freq, f_peak, Omega_0; the FDT path uses non-dimensional ANGULAR frequency, named omega, omega_0; the reduction's Omega0 is angular and non-dimensional. Hertz from the bench convert through the time unit only (core/sim_config.py:479-493).
- Units and constants. nm, ms, pN, kHz for Nadrowski; k_B = 1.380649e-23 J/K (core/config.py:219), which is 1.380649e-2 pN nm/K, so k_B T = 4.1419 pN nm at 300 K. By the code's own relation the master cells' declared f_scale = 10 pN corresponds to 63.8 K (10 * 62.14/(50 * 14.1 * 0.01380649)); the tier-1 cell at 300 K gives 46.99 pN, that is K_gs = 0.756 pN/nm.
- Three different temperatures and three more T's. temp is the motors' temperature over the bath's (a ratio); T on the tier-1 box is the bath in kelvin; T_obs, log T and T_k are recording lengths; T is also the name of the bijection in core/SBI/reparam.py. T_eff/T is the FDT ratio.
- Name collisions across models. beta is the gating energy in Nadrowski, the nonisochronicity in Hopf, and beta_H / beta-tilde in the reduction. tau (calcium relaxation) against tau_c (channel-noise time) against BP's tau_hb, tau_m, tau_gs, tau_t. s and S against BP's s_min, s_max. k against BP's k_gs (called k_gs_ratio in the constructor). chi is the susceptibility, while BP's chi_hb and chi_a are conversion factors. sigma is a noise amplitude in Hopf and sigma-squared a variance in the reduction.
- What 'the model' is numerically. The network is trained on the Euler map at dt_nd_min = 0.025 (master boxes), not on the SDE: at the master cell dt/tau = 0.93 and step times fast eigenvalue = 0.90; the calcium equation is unstable under explicit Euler for tau below dt/2 = 0.0125 while tau's box starts at 0.0001; the FDT path uses 0.01. docs/guide/science.md:42-59 says no convergence study exists. A PDF check of the equations says nothing about this unless a step is agreed.
- The solver's own step (LEAD). dt is t[1]-t[0] of a linspace over each segment's absolute end points (sdeint.py:199-200, 267-269), in float32. The numpy emulation gives exactly 0.025 in the first segment and 0.0250244 (+0.10 %) or 0.0249023 (-0.39 %) in later ones, while the drive stays on the nominal grid. Also: each segment starts from the previous one's last state, so one sample is duplicated per seam (simulator.py:92-99).
- Float precision. The engine runs float32; lock-in sums are accumulated in float64. A reference should be float64 on the CPU, and comparisons of the drift and noise should be made in float64 too (the model classes keep the dtype of the tensors they are given; HopfModel casts to its dtype argument).
- Chi normalisation. Probes are driven at a fixed non-dimensional amplitude 0.15 (dimensional 0.15*f_scale); chi is the redimensionalised response over the dimensional drive, (x_scale/f_scale) chi_nd; each row is normalised by its own lock-in duration; the mean removed is the mean of the locked-in prefix.
- FDT prefactor. T_eff/T = prefactor*omega*G/(4 chi'') with prefactor = (coefficient the drive enters the observable's drift with)/(observable's diffusion coefficient): n*beta, 2/sigma_x^2, 2 tau_hb/eta_hb^2, 1/D0.
- Reduction conventions (if in scope). Eigenvalue mu + i Omega0 with Omega0 positive; q1 scaled to q1x = 1; p1 normalised by the UNCONJUGATED product p1.q1 = 1 (np.dot), not a Hermitian inner product; alpha_H = -Re(g21)/2 and beta_H = -Im(g21)/2; whether the Hopf x is Re z or 2 Re z of the bundle displacement (with q1x = 1 the displacement is 2 Re z, and no factor 2 appears in x_scale_Hopf); absolute value of alpha under the square roots (rescaling.py:54-68); 'regime' means the sign of mu, not the criticality of the bifurcation, while a comment in rescaling.py uses 'subcritical' for the sign of alpha. At the master cell mu = +0.62 with Omega0 = 0.22, far from the bifurcation the normal form assumes.
- Transient and starting states. 100 non-dimensional time units are discarded (core/config.py:624). Training starts built-in models from random integer positions in [0, 10) and zero for the probability-like variable (core/SBI/pipeline.py:39-41, 1214-1248); observations, FDT and Simulate start from the cell file's values.
